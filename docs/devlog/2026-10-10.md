## 1. Cat landmarks: does MAE pretraining help? (cat-face-landmarks)

**Question.** The baseline (random start) plateaued at validation NME 0.0814. Does starting from the encoder that
was pretrained as a masked autoencoder on 13,061 unlabeled cat faces (CAT train + AFHQ cat train, 300 epochs)
lower it, with nothing else changed?

**Setup.** Kaggle kernel `cat-landmarks-finetune-mae`, the same script, data, settings (100 epochs, batch 64,
lr 1e-3 × batch/256, layer decay 0.75, weight decay 0.05, drop path 0.1, zoom 1–2 face widths, turn ±25°,
shift ±10%, mirror, colour jitter, seed 0) and GPU type (1 × Tesla T4, float32) as the baseline; only the
starting weights differ. Training took 210 min against the baseline's 187.

**Learning curves (validation NME).**

| epoch | 10 | 20 | 40 | 80 | 100 | best |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| from scratch | 0.1625 | 0.1274 | 0.0992 | 0.0832 | 0.0816 | 0.0814 (epoch 89) |
| from MAE | 0.1024 | 0.0874 | 0.0753 | 0.0688 | 0.0681 | 0.0681 (epoch 97) |

The MAE run passes the baseline's best at epoch 28. Both curves are flat over the last 20 epochs, so the MAE run
settles lower rather than being further along the same curve.

**Test split** (1,000 faces, never used for any choice, evaluated once per run after both had finished):

| landmark | from scratch | from MAE | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| left eye | 0.0460 | 0.0376 | −18% |
| right eye | 0.0459 | 0.0379 | −17% |
| mouth | 0.0520 | 0.0388 | −25% |
| left ear, outer base | 0.1061 | 0.0928 | −13% |
| left ear tip | 0.0724 | 0.0567 | −22% |
| left ear, inner base | 0.0950 | 0.0859 | −10% |
| right ear, inner base | 0.0951 | 0.0891 | −6% |
| right ear tip | 0.0690 | 0.0561 | −19% |
| right ear, outer base | 0.1202 | 0.1082 | −10% |
| **NME** | **0.0780** | **0.0670** | **−14%** |
| **failure rate (NME > 0.1)** | **21.4%** | **12.2%** | |

Validation agrees (NME 0.0814 → 0.0681, failure rate 19.6% → 12.6%).

**A prediction that was wrong.** Before the run I expected the largest gain on the ear bases, the points with no
edge of their own. The opposite happened: the mouth and ear tips gained most, the ear bases least. Pretraining
sharpened the points that were already well defined; the ear bases stay the hardest, which fits the earlier
suspicion that their labels themselves are noisy.

**Face by face (validation).** The MAE model has the lower error on 74% of the 1,000 faces. 114 faces fail in both
runs, 82 only from scratch, 12 only with MAE. Median face NME 0.0650 → 0.0561. The baseline's eight worst faces
(1.628, 0.715, 0.410, 0.407, 0.372, 0.371, 0.357, 0.355) score 0.191, 0.213, 0.413, 0.355, 0.293, 0.275, 0.213,
0.390 with MAE: the upside-down cat and the cat with its head turned 70–90° are no longer laid out as upright
faces, while the Scottish Fold (0.41 in both), the calico behind leaves and the crops whose labels lie outside the
photo stay bad. The MAE run's own worst face is new: a cat peering under window blinds (0.489; 0.207 from scratch),
where the slats pull the ear points down.

**Pushed** (cat-face-landmarks, as Alina CHuang, small commits): `5bafcc3` the MAE run's results and both test
evaluations, the notebook rerun with both runs on every plot, a test-set section and conclusions;
`19d03be` README results table.

## 2. AFHQ relabelled with the MAE model

**Problem.** With the baseline's labels, the ear tips of many AFHQ cats sat in the image corners. AFHQ's crops are
tight (the face fills the 512 × 512 frame), the tightest framing the model was trained on, and a soft-argmax can
never place a point outside its input.

**Change.** `label run --pad <p>` shrinks each image into the middle of a grey frame (zero after normalising, the
mean colour), so the face covers 1/p of the input as during training (p = 1.5 at evaluation), and maps the
predictions back to the image's pixels, outside it if need be. Tested with a fake model that puts every point on
the input's first and last pixel: with p = 2 on a 64-pixel image they come back at −30.5 and 93.5, as computed by
hand. Pushed as `d6e00d0`.

**What it changes** (5,558 cats; distances in eye distances, median over cats):

| landmark | baseline vs MAE (pad 1) | MAE pad 1 vs pad 1.5 | pad 1: within 8 px of the edge | pad 1.5: outside the image |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| eyes, mouth | 0.018–0.021 | 0.024–0.026 | 0% | 0% |
| ear bases | 0.034–0.043 | 0.040–0.048 | 0–0.1% | 0% |
| left ear tip | 0.032 | 0.058 | 5.5% | 25.4% |
| right ear tip | 0.032 | 0.067 | 0% | 25.1% |

At pad 1.5, 36.5% of the cats (2,029) have at least one ear tip outside the image. Drawn on a padded canvas and
inspected by eye: those tips belong to ears that the crop really cuts off at the top. Labels:
`cat-face-landmarks/data/labels/afhq_cats_mae_pad1.0.npz` and `afhq_cats_mae_pad1.5.npz`.

## 3. Can the model tell when it is wrong? Rules for the generator's data

The owner asked for the generator's training data to be filtered by objective rules (ears cut off, faces covered,
unsure landmarks), shown to the owner before use, and never to filter generated outputs. A rule for "unsure"
needs a signal that tracks the real error, so each candidate was first measured on CAT validation, where the
truth is known (MAE model, 1,000 faces, "failure" = NME > 0.1):

| signal | AUROC for failure | notes |
|---|---|---|
| mean heat-map peak ("confidence") | 0.567 | bottom 10% fail 19.0% vs 11.9% for the rest |
| lowest heat-map peak | 0.606 | |
| peak on eyes and mouth | 0.471 | worse than chance |
| **mirror disagreement** | **0.825** | correlation 0.661 with NME |

Mirror disagreement: predict the face and its mirror image, swap left and right back, and take the mean distance
between the two predictions over the eye distance. The top 20% by disagreement (above 0.036) fail 37.5% of the
time against 6.4% for the rest and hold 59.5% of all failures; the top 10% (above 0.046) fail 51.0%.

On AFHQ (MAE, pad 1.5; ear tips left out of the disagreement, since a cut-off tip is a guess either way):
median disagreement 0.033, 90th percentile 0.054, 95th 0.063. Ear tips outside the image, by how far:

| further outside than | 0 | 0.05 | 0.1 | 0.2 | 0.3 eye distances |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cats | 36.5% | 28.8% | 22.4% | 14.2% | 8.3% |

Proposed rules and the sheet the owner judged (`ml/runs/generator_filter_rules.png`; rows: rule A, the 8 highest
B, B just past a 10% threshold, kept):

- **A**, an ear tip more than 0.1 eye distance outside the image: every cat in that row has an ear cut at the top.
- **B**, mirror disagreement in the top 10%: the top of the list is heads turned sideways, flat Persian faces and
  an injured eye, but the cats just past the threshold (90–93rd percentile) are mostly ordinary frontal cats.

**Owner's decision:** A as proposed, B tightened to the top 5% (10% was too loose). A drops 1,245 cats, B 278,
both 50: **4,085 of 5,558 kept (73.5%)**. Checks file: `ml/data/processed/afhq_cats_mae_pad1.5_checks.npz`.

## 4. Painting AFHQ with the straight brush, and how many tokens a cat needs

**First version, whole frame.** `tools/paint_afhq.py` paints every cat (kept cats first, resumable), five stages
snapped to the token grid. On the Mac: median 36.5 s and L1 0.0234 over the first 27 cats; 208 painted before it
was stopped. Eight decoded from their tokens give exactly the logged L1, so the token round trip loses nothing.
A painted cat is **6,047 tokens** (1,208 strokes × 5, plus BOS, EOS and five stage marks), long for a small GPT.

**How much each stage adds** (rendered on the CPU from 60 painted cats; tokens = 2 + stages + 5 × strokes):

| stages | strokes | tokens | L1, whole canvas | L1, inside the landmark box |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | 248 | 1,245 | 0.0376 | 0.0464 |
| 1–4 | 608 | 3,046 | 0.0303 | 0.0369 |
| 1–5 | 1,208 | 6,047 | 0.0263 | 0.0318 |

The last stage (600 strokes of 10 × 4 px) costs half the tokens for an L1 drop of 0.0040.

**Face only, first try** (8 cats, CPU, 79–146 s each): cut to the landmark box plus 20%, stages 1–4. Compared
inside that box against the same target, face L1 0.0361 on average, between the whole frame's stages 1–4 (0.0388)
and 1–5 (0.0333), better than 1–5 on none of the 8 (`ml/runs/token_budget_faces.png`). Flaw: the face was cut from
the 256-pixel canvas and scaled up, so it had no more detail to paint than the whole frame.

**The owner chose the face, four stages**, cut from the 512-pixel original, and asked to see the process stage by
stage, since the earlier picture's second column (stages 1–3 together) did not look like a first step.

**Face only, from the original** (8 cats, CPU, 52–89 s each, `ml/runs/face_stages.png`): `catbrush.load(...,
box)` cuts the photo and its mask; `PaintSettings(stages=4)` stops after four stages; the box is a square around
the landmarks, 1.2 × their extent, moved and if need be shrunk to lie inside the photo. 3,046 tokens a cat;
decoding the tokens gives the same picture. L1 after each stage, mean of 8: **0.0814, 0.0609, 0.0479, 0.0386**.
Each stage now reads as a step: blocks of colour, then the head and ears, then eyes and stripes, then detail.

Honest note: AFHQ is already framed on the face, so the box is 410–500 of the 512 pixels (its top is at 0 for all
8, where the ears are); the token saving comes from dropping stage 5, not from the cut.

**Now running:** the Mac paints this version into `ml/data/processed/afhq_brush_face/` (first cat 31 s, L1 0.0267),
the 4,085 kept cats first. The 208 whole-frame paintings are kept but not used. `brushtokens.load_npz` now ignores
keys that are not stages (the saved `box`).

### 5.4 What the smoke test showed

Layer 1 (rules) and layer 3 (statistics, `review_sales.py`) over the 4 sales: all 4 sold ($85, $85, $115, $165);
282 reactions, 1 offer against its own sign and 3 over budget (1.4%); signs + 64, − 69, 0 149; 9 withdrawals, and
only 1 of 32 rounds where at least 90% of buyers reacted the same way (the herd fix works); 32 questions, all
different (most common opening "can you tell me", 6 times).

Layer 2 (the teacher as reviewer) flagged 32 of 314 items (10.2%). **I read the first sale in full** (5-year-old
Scottish Fold, farm, no vaccination records, independent, never met children) and checked its 14 flags: at most 1
holds. Two design faults in the reviewer:

- it was given the cat's hidden facts and judged buyers by them, so a buyer was "wrong" for not knowing what the
  seller had not yet said;
- it marked a seller "wrong" for not mentioning things nobody asked about.

And it missed a real fault: the seller called the cat "a laid-back farm cat" when its facts say *independent*, and
the buyers built on "laid-back" for several rounds.

The knowledge reviewer flagged 43 of 78 texts, but every one was a parse failure: when the reply held no JSON (a
sentence such as "no problems"), `_json` gave up at once instead of asking again. None was a real finding.

Fixes, each with a test:

- the reviewer now works one round at a time and sees only the conversation before that round, the answer and
  the reactions; a seller is wrong only for contradicting the facts or stating as fact something they do not say
  (the prompt names calling an independent cat laid-back); omissions are not mistakes; a reaction may use only
  what has been said;
- the seller must describe temperament and habits only as the facts do;
- `_json` asks again when a reply holds no JSON;
- the knowledge form "reverse" gets a second, natural example, after the model wrote stiff sentences of the form
  "If …, it concerns …".

## 6. Hand labels for AFHQ, larger rotations

**Hand labels.** AFHQ has no landmarks, so how well the model transfers to it can only be measured on cats
labeled by hand. `python -m catlandmarks.handlabel serve` opens a page at http://127.0.0.1:8765 with 50 cats drawn
(seeded) from AFHQ's test split, which neither pretraining nor fine-tuning saw. Each image sits in a 128-pixel grey
margin so a cut-off ear tip can be placed where it would be; the nine points are clicked in order next to a
numbered example from the CAT dataset (a frontal tabby, cut to the face); Z undoes; each cat is saved as soon as its
ninth point is placed. `handlabel score --labels <npz>` gives NME against the hand labels, divided by the
hand-placed eye distance. Clicks, saving, undo and moving on were tested in the browser; the test label was then
deleted. Pushed as `4e0678d`, with 3 tests.

**Larger rotations.** Most remaining failures are heads turned past the ±25° used in training. Kaggle kernel
`cat-landmarks-finetune-mae-turn60`: the MAE run again with ±60° and nothing else changed (the Kaggle script's
rotation is now a constant, `a9af14c`). It queued behind the dialogue run, then started while it was still
running (Kaggle allowed both at once).

### 10.2 The reviewer, read by hand

The reviewer flagged 1,432 of 6,840 items (20.9%; 1,136 wrong, 296 doubtful). Flag ids line up with events (843
name the event's buyer, 61 another buyer, 528 nobody). I read 20 random reaction flags and 10 random answer flags
against the transcript and the cat's facts:

| | flag right | item has a real problem, flag's reason wrong | item fine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| reactions (20) | 1 | 2 | 17 |
| seller answers (10) | 3 | 3 | 4 |

So the reviewer cannot be used as a filter: on reactions it is about 5–15% precise. Its common errors: calling
omissions mistakes, which the prompt says they are not; naming the wrong buyer; arguing against a reasonable
decision. Most reactions I read were sensible.

Seller answers are not errors at all: the owner's rule is that a seller may exaggerate or lie (one claimed FIV/FeLV
tests the cat never had, others said a cat gets along with other cats when nothing is known), because buyers have no
facts to check against and can only react to what was said. Only buyer reactions must be logical.

Change made: `judge_steps` now reviews only reactions. The seller's answer is shown as unnumbered context, the cat's
true facts are no longer in the prompt (they let the reviewer argue from things the buyer could not know), and the
prompt says that taking the seller at their word is correct, that sign 0 with an empty why is right when nothing on
the card is touched, and that a reaction is flagged only if no sensible buyer with that card could decide that way.
New test `test_the_reviewer_judges_only_buyers_and_never_sees_the_cats_true_facts`; 11/11 pass; ruff clean; CUPID
459 findings before and after.

## 11. Cat-dialogue handoff, compute split and painting guardrail

The 100 Qwen cat sales now have a game-model adapter (`buyers/cat_dialogues.py`). It converts only turns the
current buyer Transformer can use: on-topic cat questions become `ask`, unambiguous seller facts become `parse`,
and buyer lines with consistent sign/offer direction become `react`. The stable sale-level 80/10/10 split has
6,002 examples (4,813 train, 633 validation, 556 seen-test), and `modal_sft.py` includes it beside `buyers_v6` in
both SFT stages. The new runs are named `sft_cat_v1`/`sft_cat_v1b`, so they cannot overwrite the old `sft_v7b`,
and a cat-specific seen-test evaluation is returned before export. The Qwen reviewer remains audit metadata rather
than a training filter: hand checking found its
flag precision too low to justify deleting teacher examples.

Compute ownership:

- **Kaggle T4×2:** bulk Qwen-32B teacher dialogue/knowledge generation and review, plus sharded/restartable cat
  stroke fitting after that job is ported from MPS. These are large independent batches and fit free quota best.
- **Modal A100:** final buyer-Transformer SFT and evaluation, and later final cat-stroke Transformer training.
  These are short checkpoint-producing runs where predictable hardware and artifact return matter more than free
  hours. Modal Qwen/L40S is for small debugging batches only, not bulk teacher generation.

`paint_afhq.py` now records baseline/recent median time and MPS memory after each cat. In addition to deleting
tensors, collecting garbage and emptying the MPS cache, it aborts when the last 12 cats' median exceeds the first
24 cats' median by 2.5×. CUPID did scan this file, but none of its 45 static rules measures cross-example runtime
or GPU-memory trends; this dynamic watchdog covers that blind spot. The existing process was started before this
change, so it keeps running unchanged; its post-restart timings are currently healthy.

Six deterministic random finished cats were replayed on CPU into `docs/images/cat_painting_process.png` (photo,
24, 88, 248 and 608 cumulative strokes). Eight finished cat images are loaded from
`game/assets/stage/shelter_cats/` onto the shelter's easels; `docs/images/shelter_cats_preview.png` is an OpenGL
capture. The temporary shelter capture start position was restored to the normal x=330 afterwards.
