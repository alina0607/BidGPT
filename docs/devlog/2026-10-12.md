## 1. Brush GPT: borrowing what made the shape GPT draw eyes

The shape GPT's cats always had eyes. Four things it did that brush GPT did not: (1) the nine face points come
first; (2) each eye is its own step in the sequence; (3) faces are aligned by their points (eyes level, at fixed
pixels); (4) eye colours are clustered on their own. Tried on Modal A100s (about $1 a run), all on the 4,085
painted AFHQ cats, run D's setup otherwise:

- E: face points first (18 x/y tokens after BOS). F: the loss of strokes within 0.35 eye distances of an eye
  counts 3×. G: both.
- (2) cannot be had by reordering: strokes that overlap must keep their order, and grouping the eye strokes as
  far as that allows only takes them from 21.6 to 17.8 separate runs in the last stage (100 val cats). It would
  need the painter to paint the eyes in a pass of their own, i.e. painting every cat again.
- (3) without painting again: `brushgpt.py align` moves, turns and scales every stroke so the eyes are level, their
  midpoint at (128, 146.9) and their distance 81.9 px (the train median); size tokens are binned again. Eye
  position spread on the canvas goes from about 11 px to 0. Renders look like the original paintings, with a few
  white edges. H = D on the aligned tokens (`tokens_aligned.npz`).
- (4) is not needed: the training paintings already show clear eyes.

Judged by the cat-face landmark model's eye confidence (`ml/tools/eye_judge.py`, 128 samples each, temperature 1,
± standard error). Aligned real paintings score higher, so the fair number is generated ÷ real:

| run | real | generated | ratio | best val | eye-stroke loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D | 0.501 | 0.420 ± 0.007 | 0.84 | 3.933 | 4.036 |
| E points | 0.501 | 0.438 ± 0.007 | 0.87 | 3.917 | 3.979 |
| F eye ×3 | 0.501 | 0.434 ± 0.006 | 0.87 | 3.961 | 3.990 |
| G both | 0.501 | 0.451 ± 0.006 | 0.90 | 3.947 | 3.932 |
| H aligned | 0.537 | 0.479 ± 0.006 | 0.89 | 3.886* | 3.937 |

\* on aligned data, not quite the same task. G and H beat D by more than three standard errors, but most samples
still show no clear eyes. H's samples show more face structure (eyes and nose in a few). Run I (H + G) was stopped
after 12 minutes when the session ended and its results were lost (no Volume): to be run again.

CAT dataset, 20 cats painted on the Mac (`ml/tools/paint_cat_dataset.py`): 19 fine with clear eyes, 1 blank (mask
failed). Painting all 8,800: about 130 h on the Mac or about $23 on Modal L4s; the owner decides.

## 3. B-plan SFT data (`buyers/sales_sft.py` → `data/processed/buyers_b1/`)

One example per decision a buyer makes, prompt format 4. The prompt is what that buyer knows: their card (persona,
what they care about, budget, the offer they hold), their own opening, the earlier questions (questions only), the
last K = 2 rounds in full (who asked, the seller's answer, their own reaction) and, for a reaction, the round under
way. Never another buyer's reaction. Targets:

| task | prompt ends | target |
|---|---|---|
| open | `<|open|>` | greeting \| $offer |
| ask | `<|ask|>` | the question |
| react | `<|react|>` | sign \| what they say \| $offer or out |
| imagine | `<|imagine|>` | three things \| what they say |

`<|open|>` and `<|imagine|>` are the first two spare extra tokens renamed (same ids; nothing was trained on them).
The engine only decides whose turn it is to ask.

Split by a hash of the seed: 90 sales train, 10 val. Train 900 open, 824 ask, 7,470 react, 762 imagine; val 100,
98, 908, 89. Lengths in sp8192 tokens (prompt + target): open max 88, ask median 283 / max 581, react median 352 /
p95 611 / max 725, imagine median 457 / max 666; none over 768. ⚠️ `pretrain_large` was trained on 512-token
blocks, and 1,890 reactions, 336 scenes and 42 questions are longer than 512.

v9's ask events still hold the offer from before the hand corrections (293 of 451 differ); the reaction chain has
no break in either source, so the converter takes every offer from it.

`llm/sft.py` now takes this data: it grows the vocabulary for format 4, skips the engine-data freshness check and
the old task evaluation when the dataset has none. A 3-step run from `runs/pretrain/final.pt` on the Mac: 9,956
examples, none skipped, val loss 4.59 (untrained, as expected). Still to write before training: a held-out task
evaluation for format 4 (generate on the val prompts; offers parsed, sign agrees with the offer, within budget,
rule checks, scenes drawable).

## 4. Buyer GPT B1: first SFT and first sale in the game

60 minutes (0.9 s a step). Two false starts first: at batch 16 the Mac ran out of memory and swapped (18 GB of swap;
the new slowdown watchdog in `llm/sft.py` stopped it at step 200), and with an MPS memory cap of 0.6 the batch did
not fit. Now `sft.py` empties the MPS cache every 50 steps and validates in batches of 16.

## 6. Everything that went wrong with buyer GPT B1, and why

Versions, files and hashes: `docs/model-versions.md`.

### 6.1 In the model's lines

The one sale through the server (seed 11, 4 buyers) and the held-out evaluation show five kinds of error. The causes
below were checked against the model's raw outputs (`ml/runs/sft_b1/demo_seed11_prescripted_answers.json`) and the
training data, not guessed.

1. **A spoken amount different from the offer** (Tomas says "$60", his offer shows $50). The model wrote
   "+ | … I'll go up to $58. | $58", consistent with itself. Tomas's card budget was $50 and he had opened at $50; the
   server held the offer to the budget but left the words, so they disagreed. The real error is the model raising
   above its own budget. The training data does teach the rule (220 reactions start at the budget and all 220 keep or
   lower the offer), but following it means comparing two numbers in the prompt, which the model has not learned from
   90 sales. Openings at the full budget are rare in the data (14 of 900). Owner's decision: buyers have no budget at
   all (format 5); the server no longer holds offers.
2. **Asking again about what was answered.** Partly my test (6.3). The rest is the prompt: a buyer sees only the last
   two rounds in full and, for older rounds, only the questions. "Fully vaccinated" from round 1 was gone by round 4,
   so a question about vaccines looked new. The teachers who wrote the sales saw every question and
   answer; the student sees less, so lines that depend on old answers teach it to guess.
3. **Made-up facts and misread answers**: "At two years old" and "a 3-year-old kitten" after "She's 3 years old";
   "an overdue check-up" after "fully vaccinated"; "Being fine with the dog" after "She has never lived with dogs";
   "Having lived with a dog and loved a farm". The model stitches phrases that are frequent in the training sales:
   "Having lived with a dog" starts 14 training lines, "overdue check-up" is in 7, and the scene line "She'd ride in
   my backpack up the mountain and rest in the shade of my tent to protect her bare skin" is a training line word for
   word (a Sphynx sale). Train loss 0.41 against validation 0.91: the model memorises more than it generalises.
4. **Reasons that do not follow the card**: the hiker lowers his offer for health costs. Same cause as 3.
5. **A garbled greeting** ("the best, most exclusive and just type"): a stitched training phrase ("I collect the most
   exclusive and unusual items" is in 2 lines).

Data size, for scale: TinyStories (Eldan & Li 2023) has 2,119,719 training stories (Hugging Face
`roneneldan/TinyStories`); 200 sampled stories average 237 tokens in our sp8192 tokenizer, so about 503 M tokens. Our
100 sales hold 269,235 spoken tokens: about 1/1,870 of it, or what about 187,000 sales would hold. Our model starts
from 1.28 B tokens of pre-training, which TinyStories models did not have, so it already writes English; what it
lacks is examples of reading an answer and judging it against a card.

### 6.2 In the training run

- Batch 16 needed more than the Mac's free memory with the other apps open; macOS swapped (18 GB of swap) and steps
  slowed from 2.0 to 2.5 s. The new slowdown watchdog stopped the run at step 200, before any checkpoint (validation
  was every 200 steps). With an MPS cap of 0.6 the same batch failed at once (MPS out of memory, 14.35 GB of 14.98 GB).
  Batch 8, validation batches of 16 and an MPS cache flush every 50 steps fixed it (0.83–1.07 s a step).
- A cap needs both watermarks: `PYTORCH_MPS_HIGH_WATERMARK_RATIO=0.6` alone fails with "invalid low watermark ratio
  1.4"; `PYTORCH_MPS_LOW_WATERMARK_RATIO=0.5` too.
- Running a CPU-heavy job (the eye judge) next to training slowed the training; one job at a time on the Mac, CPU
  included.

### 6.3 My mistakes

- **The test sale used four answers written in advance that did not answer the questions** (Ines asked about health,
  the answer was "She's indoor only and calm"). Later buyers asking about health again was then reasonable, and I
  first reported it as a model error. Owner: never again. From now on, every test answer answers the question asked,
  from a fixed fact sheet, one turn at a time.
- A watcher loop used `pgrep -f "…llm.sft|eval_buyer_b1"`, which matched its own command line and would have waited
  forever; caught before it mattered and replaced by waiting on the job's process id.
- Run I's first launch was tied to the session and was stopped after 12 minutes when the session ended, results
  lost (about $0.8); run again later.
- v9's ask events still carry the offers from before the hand corrections (293 of 451 differ); found by the
  converter's own check, which now takes offers from the reaction chain (no break in either source).

### 6.4 What to do (owner decides)

- More sales: 300, then 1,000, measuring what each step fixes on the same held-out checks.
- Keep older facts in the prompt. All rounds in full needs up to 1,504 tokens and 1,536 examples exceed the model's
  1,024; questions and answers of all rounds still exceed it in 610 examples. Proposed instead: each buyer writes a
  short note of each answer (a new task learned by the model) and the prompt carries all notes plus the last two
  rounds.
- Evaluation now also checks that a spoken amount equals the offer, that an offer move is reasonable (at most 40% of
  the offer and at most 1.6 times the opening; the sales: 99.6%) and that a question is new in meaning (half of its
  content words not in one earlier question; the sales: 96.9%).

## 7. A sale from another model (Muse), seed 2100

The owner gave Muse the full generation prompt (`data/written_sales/prompt_for_external_model_seed2100.md`: no budgets,
reasonable moves, a short `note` per answer) and pasted back its sale (`data/external_sales/muse/specs/2100.json`).
It passes every program rule (`tools/write_sales.py`, written to `data/external_sales/muse/sales.jsonl`); no
unreasonable move, no repeated question, notes 8–10 words. `stories.check`/`render` now accept cards without a budget
(the 52 spec-written sales rebuild byte for byte).

| | v9 (48 sales) | spec-written (52) | Muse (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| reactions keeping the offer | 74% | 80% | 76% |
| words per reaction | 13.2 | 8.8 | 13.2 |
| keeps of the form "X is fine. $N." | 0% | **52%** | 0% |
| distinct first three words among keeps | 37% | 27% | 86% |

Read by hand: natural, specific openings and reasons; the seller honest and accurate. Faults: the same fact counted
again in later rounds (Ruby lowers for recurring vet costs in rounds 2, 8 and 9; Owen for FIV in rounds 2, 6 and 9),
against the rule that earlier facts are already in the offer; two wrong reasons (Owen: living indoors "is another
health minus"; Jonah: indoors means "fewer fights" with his own cat); Andre's scene puts the FIV-positive, indoor-only
cat outside by a barn and a fence; Leo's and Owen's scenes use things anyone has; six near-identical "the risk of a
fight doesn't apply to me" lines; cards restate the buyer's life ("retired", "has two dogs") instead of what they
want in a cat.

My own 52 sales are the weakest here on variety: half of their unchanged-offer lines are a short template, which
may be part of why B1 answers with stitched stock phrases.
