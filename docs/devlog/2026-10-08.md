## Line GPT: where B1's loss goes
No training: B1's best weights, per-token cross-entropy on the 200 held-out cats (unmirrored). Part tokens cost 0.18 nats; a coordinate costs **4.45** (uniform over 512 bins: 6.24). e^4.45 ≈ 85: the model is about as sure of each coordinate as an even guess over 85 bins of 2 px; if its guess were bell-shaped, that is a standard deviation of about 41 px, an eighth of a cat's width (cats measure a median 316 × 450 px).

| coordinates of | share of strokes | share of loss | mean loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| head | 41.8% | 41.6% | 4.42 |
| body | 36.7% | 36.8% | 4.46 |
| legs | 15.7% | 15.6% | 4.42 |
| tail | 4.7% | 4.7% | 4.53 |

(Parts come from each sketch's own part descriptions, by the first body word in them: head/ear/face/eye, torso/chest/body/back/…, leg/paw, tail.) No part is harder than another. Nor does context help much: strokes 1–4 cost 4.44 and strokes 29–32 4.48; the first stroke of a part 4.55, the sixth and later 4.40. A stroke's start point costs 4.64, its other three points 4.38. Short strokes are cheaper (4.19) than long ones (4.69).

So faces look worst not because they are predicted worse, but because the same error is large next to small features: the head's box is a median 155 × 201 px.

## Strokes that do not join
The owner's reading of the drawings: even if every cat's face differs, real strokes join into one shape, and ours do not. Measured as the distance from each stroke end to the nearest point of any other stroke (curves sampled at 40 points; 500 cats per model, seeds 2–6, t 0.8):

| | ≤ 4 px | ≤ 8 px | ≤ 16 px | median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| real held-out cats (200) | 43.2% | 66.4% | 83.8% | 4.7 px |
| A | 24.7% | 45.6% | 72.3% | 8.9 px |
| B1 | 26.8% | 49.7% | 76.2% | 8.1 px |
| B2 | 26.2% | 48.5% | 75.9% | 8.2 px |

B1 has not learned to join. Of the real cats' strokes 2–32 (part order), 2,539 start within 8 px of an earlier stroke and 2,781 more than 16 px from any. B1's loss on the start's y, once x is written, is 4.52 for the joined ones and 4.76 for the others; a model that knew the stroke starts on that earlier line would have only the few places where it crosses x left.

Why it is hard to learn: 68% of the joined starts (1,718 of 2,539) are in the middle of the earlier stroke, not at its end (821). A control point usually lies off the line, so the context holds only the strokes' end points; where a line runs in between has to be worked out from its four control points.

## Strokes as points on the curve (`--oncurve`)
A stroke can be written as the 4 points the curve passes through at t = 0, 1/3, 2/3, 1 instead of its control points: the same 8 numbers, and the same curve (solving back gives the control points to 4.5 × 10⁻¹³ px). After 2 px binning the drawn curve is off by 1.27 px on average, 1.74 px at the 99th percentile (control points: 1.17 / 1.41). What changes is what the model sees: 52% of the joined starts lie within 8 px of a point already written (control points: 37.5%; 6 points a stroke would give 63%). The model still decides whether and where to join; nothing moves its strokes. Re-running `linegpt data` left every earlier token array and number unchanged.

On-curve losses are not on the same scale as control-point losses: the map from control points to on-curve points shrinks volume by |det B| = 0.148 per axis of a stroke, so the same sketches cost 0.477 nats less per coordinate token by construction. Comparisons below convert them back (`ln|det B| = −1.9095` per axis per stroke).

## What the night shows
- **Part order + on-curve points (C and E pooled, 1,000 cats): 63.6% ± 1.5.** Against A (authors' order, control points): z = 4.65, above the Bonferroni bar for six runs (2.94). Against B1: z = 2.75, below it.

- **Joining is unsolved**: every model ends 25–30% of its strokes within 4 px of another stroke; real cats 43%. On-curve points did not change it.
- **Method**: one training run per setting cannot separate settings that are about 5 points apart. From here, a setting is judged on two training seeds.

## Stroke order inside a part: grow from what is drawn (`--order chain`)
Measured on the 200 held-out cats before any training (`order_probe`): in the authors' order a stroke's start is within 8 px of the previous stroke's end only 1.6% of the time (median 106.5 px away), and within 4 / 8 px of any earlier stroke 25.6% / 41.2% (median 12.5 px). So the order inside a part never shows the model that a new stroke starts on a drawn line. Two greedy orders, each part still opened with the authors' first stroke, a stroke allowed to be drawn backwards (same curve):

| order inside a part | start within 4 / 8 px of an earlier stroke | median | start within 8 px of the previous end |
|---|---|---|---|
| authors' (B1–E) | 25.6% / 41.2% | 12.5 px | 1.6% |
| next = nearest end to the previous stroke's end | 31.0% / 49.0% | 8.4 px | 12.4% |
| **next = nearest end to anything drawn** (`chain`) | **47.2% / 68.1%** | **4.3 px** | 7.6% |

`chain` is in `linegpt.py` (`stroke_plan`); the mirror image reuses the plan (mirroring keeps every distance). Re-running `linegpt data` (3 min) left the earlier arrays byte-identical (md5 of six arrays per split before and after); `chain` round-trips exactly, 3 part switches as in `part`.

## New data for the drawing: cat faces
The owner's reading after F: the data itself is messy. ControlSketch's real cats are SDXL images fitted with 32 strokes by optimisation, full of short hatching strokes, so even a perfect model would draw like that. Searched for public-domain drawings by one artist in one style (Hokusai's Manga, Flaxman's outlines, Ingres, Steinlen's cats, Klimt's drawings, the ARC ukiyo-e faces and KaoKore face datasets); the owner wanted faces only, with no background or accessories, and found the ukiyo-e and Japanese scroll faces ugly. Studio Ghibli was ruled out: copyrighted, with no permission for training.

**Chosen: the CAT dataset** (Zhang, Sun, Tang, "Cat Head Detection", ECCV 2008; archive.org/details/CAT_DATASET; mirrored on Kaggle as crawford/cat-dataset): about 10,000 cat photos, each with 9 head points (both eyes, the mouth, 3 points on each ear). Kaggle labels it CC0, but the authors' own page says "The CAT dataset is only for research purposes, we do not have any copyright of the images", so the CC0 label is wrong. The owner accepted research-only use; nothing derived from the photos leaves this machine (`ml/data/raw/` is ignored by git).

## Pilot: 24 faces, photo → lines → strokes
- **Fetching**: 30 photos were read out of the 954 MB zip with HTTP range requests, without downloading it. The Archive answered about half of the range requests with HTTP 500, so each request retries. Only 24 were kept: each folder numbers its photos from `00000001` again, and same-named photos overwrote each other.
- **Aligning**: rotate so the eyes are level, crop a square around all 9 points with a 20% margin. Every face came out level, ears included.
- **Lines, classical**: Canny edges and a DoG line filter (`runs/cat_faces_pilot/pilot_canny.png`, `pilot_dog.png`). Clean only on plain, single-colour cats; tabby stripes, fur and furniture became tangles.
- **Lines, Informative Drawings** (Chan, Durand, Isola, CVPR 2022; code MIT, weights `model.zip` 47.7 MB with three styles): `contour` (bold ink), `opensketch` (loose construction lines) and `anime` (fine pencil). Anime was the closest to clean line art and consistent over all 24 (`pilot_infdraw_anime_style.png`, `pilot_compare_methods.png`). The 24 faces took 8.6 s for all three styles on the CPU. The weights are loaded with `torch.load(weights_only=True)`. The network is declared again in `sketch/infdraw.py` (MIT notice in the file), because the authors' `model.py` imports torchvision, which is not installed. The released weights load into it with strict key and shape checks, and its output matches the pilot's to 0.0.

**Vectorising the anime lines did not work** (`pilot_vectorize.py`: threshold → skeleton → paths → cubic fit within 1.5 px). Coverage = share of skeleton pixels within 2 px of a fitted curve; in every version 100% of the curves lay on the skeleton, so the failures are gaps and fragments, never extra lines. The first three rows are from the printed output of runs whose files the later runs overwrote.

| version | coverage | strokes per face (median) | what went wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. threshold 0.6, junction = a pixel with ≠ 2 neighbours | 62.3% | 136 | every diagonal staircase counted as a junction and cut the line |
| 2. junctions by crossing number | 67.2% | 151 | 37.1% of skeleton pixels sat in paths under 6 px (the trace itself reached 95.3%): fur spurs cut the main lines |
| 3. drop only short paths with a free end; join paths at junctions (bend under 45°) | 75.5% | 192 | faint stretches were already gone at the threshold |
| 4. threshold 0.7 / 0.8 + 3×3 closing | 90.4% / 93.3% | 205 / 234 | dense fur merged into blobs whose skeleton is a honeycomb |
| 5. threshold 0.6 + bridging aligned free ends under 6 px | 75.6% | 183.5 | almost no change |

The owner then decided to train on the images directly (the pixel route, generated patch by patch instead of stroke by stroke), in both the contour and the anime style.

## Cat faces, pixel route (`sketch/catface.py`)
Both zips downloaded in full, md5 matching the Archive's. **Data** (561 s): 9,996 annotated photos, **8,831 kept** (the face's square at least 90% inside the photo), split 7,065 / 883 / 883 with a fixed seed, both styles at 128×128. Mirror images swap the points; which right-ear point each left-ear point becomes was read from the mean positions, `[1, 0, 2, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3]`, and checked on a sheet with the points drawn on (`runs/catface/preview_data.png`).

**Model**, as pixsketch.py: a VQ-VAE (8,248,001 parameters) turns a drawing into 16×16 = 256 codes out of 1,024, and a GPT (13,531,008 parameters, 14,130 training rows with the mirror images) learns `[BOS] x0 y0 … x8 y8 c0 … c255`, the 9 points first, so it lays out the face before drawing it.

| | anime | contour |
|---|---|---|
| VQ-VAE after 30 epochs: grey-level error | 11.89 | 19.67 |
| share of ink pixels (darker than 128) that come back as ink | **12.1%** | **80.5%** |

Anime's thin grey lines come back blurred (`runs/catface/anime/vq_recon.png`): this is the ceiling of everything the GPT draws in that style.

**Anime GPT** (20 epochs, about 311 s each): the codes' validation loss was lowest at **epoch 5 (5.0646)** and rose to 6.1538 by epoch 20 while the training loss kept falling (3.2863): it overfits from epoch 5, and the weights kept are epoch 5's. Samples (48 each, `sample_t0.8_s1.png`, `sample_t1.0_s1.png`): at t 0.8 most are clearly cat faces (two pointed ears, eyes, nose, mouth, often forehead stripes), with smudged lines; t 1.0 is messier.

| | t 0.8 | t 1.0 | real held-out |
|---|---|---|---|
| tokens of the wrong kind (a code where a point belongs, or back), total | 3 | 28 | |
| distance to the nearest training drawing (median) | 14.55 | 16.00 | 15.60 |
| variety | 22.52 | 23.42 | 27.82 |

**Memorisation check** (the owner asked whether these were really drawn by the model): generation starts from `[BOS]` alone, and training drawings are read only afterwards, for the distances. To catch a model that memorised training drawings, 48 faces were regenerated on the CPU (t 0.8) and each compared with every training row: the share of the 256 positions holding the same code as the closest training drawing has a median of **3.9%** and a max of **7.4%**, against a median of 3.1% and a max of 24.6% for 200 real held-out faces. A memorised drawing would be near 100%. Side by side with their closest training drawings they are different cats (`check_generated_vs_closest_train.png`).

There is no judge for this: the old CNN tells 15 classes apart and every drawing here is a cat. The owner set the goal as drawings people find good-looking and will judge them in blind A/B comparisons (two sheets, which model made which not shown until after the choice).

**Next, queued** (`runs/catface/run_gan.sh`, after the contour run): blur is the largest problem, so each style's VQ-VAE continues with Taming Transformers' adversarial loss (`catface vqgan`): a PatchGAN discriminator, hinge loss, the adversarial weight set each step from the gradient ratio at the decoder's last layer × 0.8. In a CPU test with a random discriminator, one step raised the reconstruction error from 0.082 to 0.193, so the discriminator now trains alone for the first epoch. The last epoch's weights are kept: blur lowers the pixel error, so the lowest-error epoch would be the blurriest. Then tokens → GPT for 10 epochs → samples, in `runs/catface/<style>_gan/`.
