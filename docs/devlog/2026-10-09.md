## Painting steps: a face as shapes laid down in order (`catshape.py`)
The owner's order: all lines first, then colour by part, then tones light to dark; colour laid at 85% opacity so
the lines show through. A face becomes 17 steps (outline, each ear, each eye, nose and mouth, forehead, each cheek,
muzzle; then base, inner ears, iris, pupil, nose colour, light tones, dark tones). Ink thinner than about 6 px
becomes centre-line strokes with a width; thicker ink stays a filled shape.

- **Data** (k 3, shapes ≥ 200 px, lines ≥ 12 px): 7,065 / 883 / 883 faces, 0 encoding errors; train tokens median
  1,404, 95th percentile 2,736, max 4,330. Vocabulary 806.
- **Round trip** on 32 faces: 96.59% of pixels within 40 grey levels of the source (min 93.26%), 0 grammar errors.
- **Where the tokens go** (train): lines 59.5% (909 a face), colour 39.3% (600), the 9 points 1.2%.

**Shape GPT** (14,471,808 parameters, sequences ≤ 3,072 tokens: 6,946 train rows, 864 validation).
- Batch 8 ran out of the GPU's memory at once. Batch 2 × 4 accumulated then ran from 03:43 to 07:30 without
  finishing an epoch: this Mac has 32 GB and MPS allows 42 GB by default, so it swapped (19.8 of 20 GB of swap
  used; 68 CPU-minutes in 3 h 46 min).
- Batch 1 × 8 with the MPS cap at 15 GB (`PYTORCH_MPS_HIGH_WATERMARK_RATIO=0.6`) failed within a minute: every new
  sequence length kept its own buffers. Padding lengths up to multiples of 512 and freeing the cache every step
  fixed it: the 40 longest sequences interleaved with random ones peaked at 12.2 GB; 2.84 s a step, 43 min an epoch.
- 8 epochs: validation loss 2.4417 → **2.0120** (epochs 7 → 8 gained 0.0011).
- 16 samples: t 0.8 all ended with EOS, 7 grammar errors in total, median 1,291 tokens, 17 steps; t 1.0: 1,035
  grammar errors, several half-drawn faces. Sampling had crashed on a stroke token with no points; decode now
  counts it as a grammar error.

The samples follow the 17 steps in order and get head shape, ears and eye placement right, but the lines are
scattered and the colour is hard-edged polygons, far below the training faces themselves. **Owner's reading: the
training drawings are ugly, so the samples can be no better; the next data should come from sharper photos.**

**Colour only, finer tones** (same 5 validation faces): lines + colour (k 3) median 1,661 tokens; colour only k 3:
622; k 6 (shapes ≥ 80 px): 2,043; k 10 (≥ 40 px): 3,882. On 300 validation faces, colour only: k 6 median 1,843,
max 3,481, 1.7% over 3,072; k 10 median 3,683, max 6,046, **73.7% over 3,072, none over 6,144**.

## Game sprite: a drawn cat's face on a shared body (`catbake.py`, `catsprite.py`)
Sheets drawn with ChatGPT from the brief in design-v2 §14.6 (colourless frames + a row with one flat colour per
part). The face is warped onto the sprite's head with a piecewise affine map pinned at the eyes and mouth and at 50
outline pairs: the 6 ear points are moved onto each outline and each stretch between them is divided by length.
Equal-angle rays (the first version) matched ear to cheek wherever the photo's ears and the sprite's point differently,
and folded the triangles into streaks. The body is quilted from 10 px patches of the cat's own fur.

Head share of the cat's area, measured on the label rows: chibi sheet 64%, realistic sheet 9.6%; at the same
height the realistic head is 0.59 of the chibi's width. The realistic sheet keeps its lines and shading under the
fur (the frame's grey over its median fill multiplies the texture).

## Cat landmarks, now their own repository
The 9-point finder moved to [cat-face-landmarks](https://github.com/alina0607/cat-face-landmarks) so AFHQ's cats can be
labeled: ViT-Small (21.6M) pretrained as a masked autoencoder on 13,061 unlabeled faces (CAT train + AFHQ cat
train; 300 epochs on the Mac, held-out loss 1.2407 → 0.4723), then a ViTPose-style decoder with soft-argmax.
Baseline from random weights (Kaggle T4, 100 epochs, 187 min): validation NME 0.0814, failure rate 19.6%; eyes
0.044, ear outer bases 0.111 / 0.117. The MAE-initialised run is on Kaggle with identical settings. All 5,558 AFHQ
cats are labeled with the baseline for now: eyes and mouths look right, ear tips are often pushed into the frame's
corners.

## Brush strokes (`catbrush.py`, `brushtokens.py`)
The owner chose a new representation for the generator: an ordered list of brush strokes, big to small, fitted to
the photo through a differentiable renderer (Stylized Neural Painting's approach). 5 stages, 1,208 strokes
(24 / 64 / 160 / 360 / 600, 96×40 px down to 10×4 px on a 256 canvas), 150 Adam steps a stage. On one AFHQ cat
(straight brush): L1 to the photo 0.0473 after stage 1, **0.0229** after stage 5; 11 min 27 s on the CPU,
187 s on the Mac GPU. Four styles are written (straight, curved Bezier, brush texture, watercolour); the
comparison on 5 cats is running on Kaggle. Time a cat on the Mac GPU: straight 3.0 min, Bezier 19.0, texture 2.7,
watercolour 3.4.

**Tokens**: X, Y, ANGLE, SIZE, PAINT (colour + opacity), plus SHAPE for the curved brush: 5 tokens a stroke, 6,047
for the cat above. Quantising one field at a time: position at 2 px +0.0028, colour at 8 levels +0.0029, size
+0.0015, angle +0.0007, opacity +0.0004 (all fields: 0.0307). With 1 px positions, 12 colour levels and each stage
snapped to its token bins before the next one paints (so later strokes cover the snapping error): **0.0256**, and
repainting from the tokens gives exactly that canvas. Vocabulary 7,531.

The owner approved filtering the generator's training data (ears cut off, faces covered, unsure landmarks) by
fixed rules, with the would-be-rejected cats shown first; generated samples are never filtered.
